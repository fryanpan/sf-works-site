# Crash fixes: schema and counting rule

`lanes/` holds one JSON file per research lane (area, bike, crosscut, junction, pedestrian, speed), a completeness critique (`critique.json`) and a follow-up (`fill.json`) that adds fixes, adds group mappings, corrects misfits and adds costs. [`../fix-map.py`](../fix-map.py) merges them and writes `out/crash-augment/profiles/fix-map.json`:

    python3 analysis/crash-profiles/crash-profiles.py
    python3 analysis/crash-profiles/fix-reach.py
    python3 analysis/crash-profiles/fix-map.py
    python3 analysis/crash-profiles/fix-groups.py
    python3 analysis/crash-profiles/fix-explorer.py

Standard library only. A second run writes the same bytes.

## Years

Every count is collision years 2018 to 2025, eight years, and a year is the count over 8. The range is set once, as `YEARS` in [`../crash-profiles.py`](../crash-profiles.py), and the other four scripts read it from `results.json`. The lanes' `target_share` texts quote 2017 to 2024 counts, the window they were researched on. [`lane-counts.json`](lane-counts.json) holds those counts (written once with `fix-map.py --freeze-lane-counts` from a 2017 to 2024 `results.json`), so an "A of B" text whose B is a 2017 to 2024 group total is still read as the share A / B and applied to the 2018 to 2025 counts. Shares from `reach.json` places are recounted on the current window.

## Schema

Each lane file is `{"lane": "...", "fixes": [...], "gaps": ["..."]}`. Each fix:

| Field | Holds |
| --- | --- |
| `id` | kebab-case, stable |
| `name`, `how_it_works` | the treatment and one sentence on how it works |
| `lever` | one of street design, signals, speed management, traffic volume and filtering, pricing, vehicles and fleets, automated enforcement, behaviour programs, transit and land use, policy and law |
| `groups` | one entry per crash group it reaches: `group` as `"<mode> \| <family>"` from `size_families` in `out/crash-augment/profiles/results.json` (or a cross-cut `place \|`, `who \|`, `time \|` group), optional `movement`, `mechanism`, `target_share` (the share of the group's people hurt it can reach, with its basis, as text) and `effect` |
| `effect` | `low`, `mid`, `high` in the study's own unit, `unit`, `applies_to` (which crashes and which severity), `source`, `tag` |
| `speed_change` | optional, on a group entry: a measured or cited change in speed, as `before`, `after` and `unit`, or as `low`, `mid`, `high` in % change; `measure` (mean, median or 85th percentile), `use` (`effect`, `bounds` or `check`, see the power model below), `source`, `tag` |
| `unit`, `cost` | the unit of treatment; `cost` is `low`, `high`, `year`, `unit`, `source`, `tag` in US dollars |
| `life_years` | service life, for a one-time cost |
| `sf_now`, `sf_evidence`, `who_decides`, `evidence_grade`, `sources` | what SF does now, SF's own result, who decides, the grade with its reason, and each source with its URL and provenance tag |

`fill.json` records also carry `fill_action`: `new`, `add_groups` (append groups to the record named in `extends`), `misfit_corrected` (replace the named groups or fields) or `cost_added` (set the cost).

## How the merge counts

- **Same treatment, one record.** `ALIASES` in the script maps duplicate ids to one canonical id (for example `congestion-pricing` and `congestion-pricing-cordon` to `area-cordon-congestion-charge`). The canonical record's fields lead and the aliases add their group mappings. Where a fix maps the same group twice, the first entry with a usable effect and share is used and the others are kept as alternates.
- **Effects in one unit.** Every effect becomes r, the fraction of the target crashes removed: `% fewer` is v/100, `% change` is -v/100, a CMF or rate ratio is 1 - v. An increase stays negative.
- **Applied only where it fits.** An effect is used only if it is a crash or injury reduction (not speeding, violations, yielding, an exponent, a hazard, a share of crashes, or a per-trip injury odds ratio from emergency department studies), only where its own `applies_to` does not rule it out (a rural design, a rural clear zone), and only for the road user it names. A fix whose remaining effects are all harmful, because the studies of its benefit were dropped for their unit, is not counted: its figure would be the side effect alone (`curb-protected-bike-lane`, whose studies measure injury risk per trip, not crashes). Where an entry's value is a manual one that is not about its group, the record's own value is used (`EFFECT_FROM_GROUP`: the road diet's red-light entry takes its corridor CMF 0.81).
- **Speed changes through the power model.** An entry's `speed_change` is turned into a cut for each severity, 1 - (v_after / v_before)^p, with the urban exponents of Trafikverket's [Normalvärden och förbättringsåtgärder trafiksäkerhet](https://bransch.trafikverket.se/contentassets/6172806b6959485d895374173243047f/2026/normalvarden-och-forbattringsatgarder-trafiksakerhet.pdf) (2026, Table 42, pp. 66 to 67): killed 3.8, seriously injured 2.6, slightly injured 1.4. The low end takes 1.6 for killed or seriously injured, Cameron and Elvik's (2010) value for urban arterials, read in [Frith and Tate 2024, Table 1](https://wsp.com/-/media/insights/new-zealand/documents/paper---using-speed-crash-models-appropriately.pdf). A KSI person is killed or seriously injured in SF's 2022 to 2024 grade mix (11.7% killed). The power model never adds to a study: `use: effect` makes it the entry's effect only where the entry's own effect is not a crash outcome; `bounds` keeps the study's mid and widens its low and high to the power model's; `check` puts it beside the entry's effect and applies nothing. Every entry today is a `check`: humps, feedback signs, signed 20 mph limits, signal retiming, and the hardened centerline's 17% slower mean left turns, whose vehicle left-turn entry stays not computed because the exponents come from mean traffic speed on a road, not the turning speed at one corner. `counting.power_model` in `fix-map.json` lists each entry with both effects. An 85th-percentile change stands in for the mean only in `check` entries. The NYC fleet speed-limiter study reports time spent speeding, not a mean speed, so ISA has no `speed_change`.
- **Severity per value.** Each of an effect's low, mid and high is read for severity from the unit, and from `applies_to` and `source` where they tie a value to a severity ("Mid: London KSI -12%", "(high: killed)", "pedestrian severe injuries (0.67)"). A value on deaths or serious injuries alone ("fatal and serious", "KSI", "killed", "crash deaths") applies to KSI people only. People hurt take the record's all-injury values, a KSI-only value giving way to the nearest smaller all-injury one. An effect with no all-injury value removes KSI people only, and they count as people hurt avoided too, since every KSI person is a person hurt.
- **Reach from the crash map, for the fixes `reach.json` places.** [`reach.json`](reach.json) maps a fix to the kind of place it goes (signalized corners, unsignalized arterial corners, two-way stops, the High Injury Network, a street class, multi-lane arterials and collectors, candidate low-traffic cells, speed camera corridors and others, or an expression over them) and to SF's count of those places. [`../fix-reach.py`](../fix-reach.py) tags every crash in the window with those places and writes each group's people hurt and KSI people in each, by movement, with SF's counts and their sources, to `out/crash-augment/profiles/fix-reach.json`. A placed fix's share of a group is the group's people in that place (within the entry's movement) over the group's total, and its per-unit cost is multiplied by the place count, so the cost and the reach cover the same places. Where the lane's share names a party or crash type rather than a place (driver at fault, sideswipes), `"text": "multiply"` keeps it and multiplies by the place's fraction. A record with no `life_years` takes the Caltrans Local Roadway Safety Manual's Expected Life for the nearest countermeasure, named in `reach.json`. `not_placed` there lists the fixes left out and why. Miles count a divided street once and a divided street's two crossings count as one corner (the rule in `tools/street-safety/hin-densest-stretches.py`), because SF's centerline file draws many arterials twice: arterial miles fall from 138.5 drawn to 86.4. Run `fix-reach.py` before `fix-map.py` after editing `reach.json`.
- **Sites already treated are netted out where SF has a count.** `treated` in `reach.json` takes out a fraction of both the crashes reached and the places priced (LPIs: SFMTA's 81% of signals with an LPI or no turn conflict, a ceiling; daylighting: the middle of SFMTA's 57% to 65%), or leaves treated places out of the reach (today's camera corridors, Slow Streets, blocks posted 20 mph, HIN blocks at 20 mph). A placed fix with no count of what is done says `most it could reach` on its record (`reach_treated`) and its ranking row.
- **Signal timing priced with its hardware.** For the LPI, the exclusive pedestrian phase, retiming, all-red and yellow change, `reach.json`'s `cost` replaces the lanes' labour-only price with a range from labour ($100 a signal) to engineering and hardware: $50,000 for an LPI (Vision Zero SF's band), $150,000 for an exclusive phase (FHWA PEDSAFE), $10,000 for timing changes (Vision Zero SF). A signal that needs a new cabinet or heads costs a full upgrade, $424,000 (Public Works' Contract 35 award) to $1.18M (SFCTA's allocation for the same 17 sites) a location, which is not priced.
- **Upkeep.** `upkeep` adds a yearly cost per unit: the low-traffic cells carry the record's filter upkeep per mile of cell street.
- **Low confidence on reach.** At the corners the stop sign layer calls uncontrolled, police code a working control device in 48% of crashes, and 46% to 59% at stop-controlled ones (86% at signals), so `reach_confidence` is low for the beacon, all-way stop, stop warning beacon and mini-roundabout fixes (`checks.police_control_device_by_node_class` in `fix-reach.json`).
- **SF's own results set the low end.** Where SF has tested a treatment on its own streets (20 mph blocks, road diets, quick-build corridors, painted safety zones, speed humps, speed cameras, Slow Streets), `SF_EVIDENCE` in the script sets the low to no effect when SF's 95% range includes no change, or to SF's result when it does not, wherever the lane's low is more hopeful. Each pair carries `sf_evidence` saying whether it changed.
- **Target share from the record, for every other fix.** An explicit "N% of the group" wins. Otherwise the share is "A of B" where B is the group's own total. If the text says the reachable share is not computed, unknown, or limited to some blocks, corridors or sites, the pair is not computed, because the street-class counts beside such a caveat overstate the reach.
- **Avoided a year**, crash by crash. Each entry reaches a set of crashes in its family: a placed fix's place and movement, or, for a share read from text, the crashes the text names where a crash field records them (the at-fault party, sideswipes or broadsides, solo crashes, the street classes it lists, the movement it opens with), else the entry's movement, else the whole family, the first set that holds the share. On each crash it reaches the entry removes rho = share x r / f, where f is the fraction of the family's people those crashes hold, so a placed fix removes r there. rho is at most 1, so an entry never removes more than everyone in a crash. A harmful entry has no floor: a study's rise in crashes, such as the mini-roundabout's bike rate ratio of 3.4, is applied in full. Each crash's KSI people and other people hurt are kept apart, and a fix's people hurt avoided are its KSI people avoided plus its other people hurt avoided, over 8 years. A row's own figure in the groups list is group people / 8 x share x r.
- **Annual cost.** A per-year cost is used as is and a one-time cost is spread over `life_years`. A per-unit cost (per corner, mile, signal, camera) needs SF's unit count, which `UNITS` takes from the record's own text or from `results.json`. With no count, or no life for a one-time cost, the cost per injury is not computed. Dollar years are as the records give them.
- **Cost per injury avoided**: low = cost low / avoided high, mid = cost mid / avoided mid, high = cost high / avoided low. The value of injuries avoided is counted per KSI person and per other person hurt, each weighted by SF's 2022 to 2024 police grade mix, two ways, each giving a benefit-cost ratio. The SF Budget and Legislative Analyst's [2025 crash costs](https://sfbos.archive.sf.gov/sites/default/files/BLA.Traffic_Crash_Costs.042325.pdf) are economic only: a death $1,604,902 (Exhibit 8); a severe injury $356,117, the average of Exhibit 5's NHTSA costs for MAIS 3 to 5 ($280,726, $675,727, $979,328) over their national counts; any other injury $24,514, the same average for MAIS 1 and 2 ($19,344, $71,419). That puts a KSI person at $593,744 and another person hurt at $28,980 in 2024 dollars, brought from 2019 dollars by Exhibit 8's own totals. Reading police grades as MAIS bands is our inference: the BLA itself used one $36,949 average because SF's grades don't align with MAIS. USDOT's 2026 comprehensive values are by police grade already. Caltrans' Local Roadway Safety Manual prices crashes, not people, and is not used.
- **Ranking.** Each fix is ranked on its own, by cost per person hurt avoided at mid. Fixes acting on the same group are not added together in the ranking.
- **Enablers.** A law that only allows another fix (camera-enforced filters, lifting the camera cap, civil red-light penalties) is not ranked; its benefit and cost sit with the fix it allows.

## Stacking

Each fix does a little, so the script also builds stacked packages and counts them sequentially, the rule in TRL's PPR844, crash by crash: each crash keeps the fraction of its KSI people and of its other people hurt that the fixes before have left, and an added fix removes its rho of what is left on every crash it reaches. Fixes that reach the same crashes, or nested places such as signalized arterial corners inside arterial corners, are never credited with more people than those crashes hold; what a crash keeps never falls below none, and harm is counted in full. The families include the crashes whose mode is not stated, so the package total over them holds everyone hurt. An `all other families` entry is applied to each family outside the chart unless the fix maps that family itself. Cross-cut groups overlap the families and are reported but not stacked. `CONFLICTS` lists fixes that are versions of one another (a citywide 20 mph default and street-by-street limits, a camera network and today's cameras) or alternatives at one place, each priced at every place of its kind (the left-turn ban, curbed median, left-turn pocket, hardened centerline and protected left-turn phase at an arterial corner; a raised or a high-visibility crosswalk at an unsignalized crossing), and a stack takes only the first of each. After every stack the script checks that no crash or family has more people avoided than it holds.

Sequential counting assumes the fixes' effects are independent. It never credits two fixes with the same person, though it can still over-credit fixes that work through the same mechanism.

The stacks in `fix-map.json`:

- `greedy_by_people_hurt`: adds the fix with the lowest cost per person hurt avoided on what remains, until every computable fix is in. Each step records the cumulative people hurt and KSI avoided, cumulative annual cost and the share of all SF people hurt and KSI avoided, so a tool can draw share avoided against cumulative cost.
- `greedy_by_ksi`: the same, ranked by cost per KSI person avoided.
- `area_wide_only`, `corner_and_corridor_only`, `area_wide_and_corner_corridor`: the greedy stack within each kind.
- Every step of these stacks also carries its cumulative people avoided with every effect at its low and at its high, in the same order.
- `greedy_by_people_hurt_optimistic` and `_pessimistic`: `greedy_by_people_hurt`'s fixes in its order with every effect at its high and every cost at its low, and every effect at its low (harmful ones included) and every cost at its high.

## Counting crash by crash, for any grouping

[`../fix-groups.py`](../fix-groups.py) recounts every fix and every stack step from `fix-map.json`'s `stack_terms` on its own reading of the crashes, and sums them by any grouping of the same crashes (who was hurt, place, time, crash type, speed limit, lanes, light, Equity Priority Community, heaviest vehicle weight, and the rest in `GROUPINGS` in `crash-profiles.py`), so every grouping shows each fix's share:

- For each term it finds the crashes reached and recomputes rho from the term's share, effect and those crashes. The script stops unless every rho and crash count equals `fix-map.json`'s, and unless the sum over every crash equals each fix's people hurt and KSI people avoided at low, mid and high. Nothing is scaled to make them match, so a counting error on either side stops the run.
- A person grouping splits a crash's KSI people avoided over its KSI victims and its other people hurt avoided over its other hurt victims.
- Every grouping's groups must add to each fix's total, and no crash or group may have more KSI people or other people hurt avoided than it holds, for every fix and every stack step, or the script stops.
- Stacks are replayed step by step at mid on the same crashes; each step's sum must equal its marginal in `fix-map.json`.

It writes `out/crash-augment/profiles/fix-groups.json`: every group of every grouping with its people hurt and KSI people, each fix's people avoided there (low, mid, high), and each stack's people avoided there at each step.

## Limits

- A placed fix is costed and credited at every place of its kind not yet treated (every signal, every unsignalized arterial corner, every HIN mile, less what SF has counted as done). Where SF has no count it is the most the fix could reach, not a program SF has proposed, and the cost per person hurt is the same at any fraction of those places only if the places are alike.
- Fixes still not computable mostly lack an effect size on crashes, a cost, or a countable unit; `not_computable` lists every pair and why, and `reach.not_placed` the fixes `reach.json` leaves out.
- The stacked totals are only as wide as the computable fixes, so they understate what a full package could reach.
- Effects are borrowed from studies elsewhere and applied at SF's crash counts; the evidence grade on each record says how far to trust each one.
- A share read from text that names no kind of crash a field records (the congestion charge's downtown zone) is spread over its whole family, so the crashes it removes are the family's average ones.

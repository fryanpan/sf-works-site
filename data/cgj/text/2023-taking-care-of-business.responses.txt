 3   Resolution responding to the Presiding Judge of the Superior Court on the findings

 4   and recommendations contained in the 2022-2023 Civil Grand Jury Report, entitled

 5   "Taking Care of Business: San Francisco's Plan to Save its Small Businesses;" and

 6   urging the Mayor to cause the implementation of accepted findings and

 7   recommendations through her department heads and through the development of the

 8   annual budget.

 9

10           WHEREAS, Under California Penal Code, Section 933 et seq., the Board of

11   Supervisors must respond, within 90 days of receipt, to the Presiding Judge of the Superior

12   Court on the findings and recommendations contained in Civil Grand Jury Reports; and

13           WHEREAS, In accordance with California Penal Code, Section 933.05(c), if a finding or

14   recommendation of the Civil Grand Jury addresses budgetary or personnel matters of a

15   county agency or a department headed by an elected officer, the agency or department head

16   and the Board of Supervisors shall respond if requested by the Civil Grand Jury, but the

17   response of the Board of Supervisors shall address only budgetary or personnel matters over

18   which it has some decision making authority; and

19           WHEREAS, Under San Francisco Administrative Code, Section 2.1 O(a), the Board of

20   Supervisors must conduct a public hearing by a committee to consider a final report of the

21   findings and recommendations submitted, and notify the current foreperson and immediate

22   past foreperson of the Civil Grand Jury when such hearing is scheduled; and

23           WHEREAS, In accordance with San Francisco Administrative Code, Section 2.1 O(b),

24   the Controller must report to the Board of Supervisors on the implementation of

 6   Resolution as if set forth fully herein; and

 7           WHEREAS, The Civil Grand Jury has requested that the Board of Supervisors respond

 9   contained in the subject Report; and

10           WHEREAS, Finding No. F1 states: "First Year Free has been generally well-received

11   by small businesses and City agencies, but due to its limited scope when first adopted and its

12   status as a temporary pilot program, its financial benefits to small businesses have been more

13   modest than initially expected;" and

14           WHEREAS, Finding No. F9 states: "Despite recent reforms, there remains a prevalent

15   perception that San Francisco is inhospitable to small businesses, and City agencies have not

16   deployed the resources required to effectively counter that perception;"

17           WHEREAS, Recommendation No. R1 states: "By November 30, 2023, the Mayor and

18   Board of Supervisors should take the legislative and budgetary steps necessary to make First

19   Year Free a permanent program.;" and

20           WHEREAS, Recommendation No. R5 states: "By November 30, 2023, the Board of

21   Supervisors should resolve to allow an additional period of three years (from December 2023

22   through December 2026) before initiating or considering proposals to delete or amend the

23   provisions of Proposition H (as defined in this report) requiring 30-day permit processing and

24   elimination of neighborhood notification for eligible commercial projects.;" and

 1          WHEREAS, Recommendation No. R7 states: "By March 1, 2024, the Mayor and Board

 2   of Supervisors should take the legislative and budgetary steps necessary for the City to

 3   implement a cross-departmental technology integration strategy for the deployment of a

 4   federated computer system that will increase data visibility across all departments involved in

 5   business registration, planning, and permitting, for improved collaboration and to enable

 6   compilation of disparate system data into transparent and usable output for small business

 7   customers.;" and

 8          WHEREAS, Recommendation No. R8.5 states: "By December 31, 2023, each member

 9   of the Board of Supervisors should conduct meetings on at least a quarterly basis with small

10   businesses and merchants' associations within his or her district, to address issues impacting

11   small business formation and operation and potential solutions, and to disseminate

12   information (with appropriate contact information for City personnel) regarding small business

13   assistance programs such as First Year Free, Proposition H, and any materials developed in

14   response to R8.2 and R8.3 above. Each Supervisor should include a summary report of each

15   such meeting in any publication or newsletter issued by his or her Office and provide a copy to

16   OSB;" and

17           WHEREAS, Recommendation No. R9 states: "By January 31, 2024, the Mayor and

18   Board of Supervisors, in consultation with OSB, should approve funding sufficient to develop

19   and launch a sophisticated marketing campaign to promote San Francisco as a welcoming

20   and supportive environment for new small businesses to open, and to encourage support for

21   existing small businesses, with reference as appropriate to programs such as First Year Free

22   and Proposition H;" and

24   Supervisors must respond, within 90 days of receipt, to the Presiding Judge of the Superior

 2   contained in the subject Report; now, therefore, be it

 3           RESOLVED, That the Board of Supervisors reports to the Presiding Judge of the

 4   Superior Court that they agree with Finding No. F1; and, be it

 5           FURTHER RESOLVED, That the Board of Supervisors reports to the Presiding Judge

 6   of the Superior Court that they partially disagree with Finding No. F9 for the following

 7   reason: it is critical to reverse existing perceptions that the City is inhospitable to small

 8   businesses, including conducting more outreach about the programs small businesses can

 9   utilize for their benefit. However, the City agencies have deployed resources to counter this

10   perception, and we do not have certainty that it is not the level of resources required to

11   counter the perception; and, be it

12           FURTHER RESOLVED, That the Board of Supervisors reports that Recommendation

13   No. R1 requires further analysis and requests the Budget and Legislative Analyst's Office to

14   study the financial impacts of making First Year Free a permanent program and provide a

15   report by June 30, 2024; and, be it

16           FURTHER RESOLVED, That the Board of Supervisors reports that Recommendation

17   No. RS has not been implemented but will be implemented by June 30, 2024; and, be it

18           FURTHER RESOLVED, That the Board of Supervisors reports that Recommendation

19   No. R7 requires further analysis and requests the Budget and Legislative Analyst's Office to

20   study the financial and process impacts of making departments, including the Department of

21   Building Inspection, move to a new computer system and provide a report by September 30,

22   2024; and, be it

23           FURTHER RESOLVED, That the Board of Supervisors reports that Recommendation

24   No. R8.5 will not be implemented because it is not reasonable since each Supervisorial

25   district is unique and contains different institutions, non-profits, merchant associations, and




     Government Audit and Oversight Committee
     BOARD OF SUPERVISORS                                                                            Page4
 1   community stakeholders, and that most offices already hold these meetings regularly; and, be

 2   it

 3           FURTHER RESOLVED, That the Board of Supervisors reports that Recommendation

 4   No. R9 has been implemented with existing resources dedicated to marketing and promoting

 5   the City's supportive environment for small businesses through the ShopDineSF campaign

 6   and raising awareness through information videos and handouts of Proposition H's as well as

 7   First-Year-Free's provisions to small business applicants at the permit center and online; and,

 8   be it

 9           FURTHER RESOLVED, That the Board of Supervisors urges the Mayor to cause the

10   implementation of the accepted findings and recommendations through her department heads

11   and through the development of the annual budget.

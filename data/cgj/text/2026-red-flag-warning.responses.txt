   Dear Judge East,

   In accordance with Penal Code 933 and 933.05, the following is in response to the 2025-2026
   Civil Grand Jury Report, Red Flag Warning – Wildfire Risk in San Francisco’s Glen Canyon. We would like
   to thank the members of the 2025-2026 Civil Grand Jury for their interest in our City’s wildland
   parks and ensuring their long-term protection from fire risk.

   We agree with the core intent of the Jury’s findings, to bring attention to the risk posed by fire in
   wildland areas of the City including Glen Canyon. The City also recognizes that Glen Canyon poses
   a unique challenge because multiple departments with differing scopes of responsibility and missions
   have adjacent and overlapping jurisdictions. The City strives for operational efficiency to maximize
   the impact of limited resources and is open to considering improvements that have the potential to
   further reduce the risk of fire, within existing funding levels.

   The City does, however, disagree with some of the Jury’s findings and recommendations about City
   departments’ general preparedness for fires in wildland areas. The three City departments with
   jurisdiction in Glen Canyon perform responsible vegetation management within the scope of their
   operational duties that reduce fire fuel. In addition, the San Francisco Fire Department mitigates
   risks through drills, wildland firefighting apparatus, and pre-planning for fires in wildland urban
   interface. Considering changes in allocating responsibility requires analysis by departments. In
   addition, at a general citywide level and specific to Glen Canyon, CAL Fire rates San Francisco as a
   “low” risk fire rating.

   We commend the Jury’s desire to increase sharing of technology and best practices to improve lines
   of communication between departments with adjacent and overlapping wildland jurisdictions.
   Further, we thank the Jury for raising the importance of these departments coordinating with the
   Fire Department to ensure preparation for any wildland fire.

   The City takes these findings and recommendations seriously and we will continue to ensure
   wildlands are appropriately assessed for fire hazard and risks are mitigated.

   We are including our detailed responses from the Mayor’s Office, the Recreation and Parks
   Department, the Fire Department, San Francisco Public Works, and the San Francisco Public
   Utilities Commission. Please note, the Urban Forestry Council ceased to exist on June 30, 2026, so
   we have not included a response from that body.

13           WHEREAS, Recommendation No. R5.2 states: "By June 30, 2027, the UFC should

15   Phase 2, "Trees in Parks and Open Spaces" of the Urban Forest Plan,47 with specific focus

16   on invasive species management and wildfire abatement;" and

17           WHEREAS, In accordance with California Penal Code, Section 933.05(c), the Board of

20   subject Report; now, therefore, be it

21           RESOLVED, That the Board of Supervisors reports to the Presiding Judge of the

22   Superior Court that they partially agree with Finding No. F1. The Board agrees that the aging

23   eucalyptus canopy poses a risk of collapse during storms, and that the accumulation of bark

24   and leaf litter and the ladder fuels documented in the Report present a hazard warranting

25   ongoing attention. The Board disagrees that this condition rises to a material wildfire danger

 1   citywide. The Report's assessment of vegetation conditions is drawn from Glen Canyon, and

 2   CAL Fire's Fire Hazard Severity Zone map, which classifies land based on fuel, terrain, and

 3   fire weather, does not place the City's wildland areas in a high or very high fire hazard severity

 4   zone; and, be it

 5           FURTHER RESOLVED, That the Board of Supervisors reports to the Presiding Judge

 6   of the Superior Court that they agree with Finding No. F5. The Board notes that the Council

 7   engaged with wildfire risk in Glen Canyon through site tours, expert presentations, and a

 8   survey of member agencies' practices, but was unable to advance that work into guidance or

 9   practice standards because its urban forest planning work was paused for lack of funding,

10   data, and staff capacity; and, be it

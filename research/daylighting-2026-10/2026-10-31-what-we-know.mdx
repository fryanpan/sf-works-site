# Daylighting and Corner Fixes: What We Know

**Daylighting** keeps the last 20 feet before a crosswalk clear of parked cars, so drivers and people crossing can see each other. **Hardened** means something physical sits in that space. The **High Injury Network** is the streets with the most severe injuries. **Turn calming** is posts and rubber bumps that force slower, tighter left turns. **Equity Priority Communities** are the tracts the regional planning agency marks for low-income residents and people of color [model knowledge — verify]. **Prop L** is SF's half-cent transportation sales tax [model knowledge — verify].

## Summary for the Decision Makers

| Goal                        | Concern or hurdle                                            | Recommendation                                               |
| --------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------ | ------------------------------------------------------------ |
| Fewer people hit at corners | The Civil Grand Jury warns that "simply painting curbs red may not be sufficient". No study has tested red paint; 567 New York corners with signs only showed no change | Put something physical first at High Injury Network corners: posts, bike share docks or turn calming, ahead of rebuilt curbs |
| A pace the public can check | SFMTA reports percentages with no list; Supervisor Melgar's 2023 hearing request for a count ([File 231031](https://sfgov.legistar.com/LegislationDetail.aspx?ID=6373712&GUID=F498D7E1-6E28-4B8C-A11F-F750DE0617CD&FullText=1)) lapsed unheard in 2025 [Legistar — read 2026-10-31] | Ask the SFMTA Board for a yearly hardened corner target and a public list of every corner, its treatment and its date |
| Win the parking fight       | Supervisor Wong, Sunset shop owners and a repeal petition point to lost parking; nobody has counted it by block, and we found no rollout that avoided the fight | Publish spaces lost per corridor before each phase, and keep commercial loading by ordinance where shops need it |
| Keep fines fair             | The Board of Supervisors said painted zones protect families from "predatory fees" ([Res. 632-24](https://sfbos.archive.sf.gov/sites/default/files/r0632-24.pdf), p. 2), and red zone tickets rose from 12,916 to 28,542, January to September 2024 against 2026 | Keep warnings at unpainted curbs and report tickets monthly by district and Equity Priority Community |
| Show it worked              | The repeal petition asks for "a formal authoritative analysis", and SF has only one test, in the Tenderloin, which can't rule out no effect | Test SF's 286 painted safety zones now, and name comparison corners before the next phase |

**In short:**

- **Does it work?** Clearing the corner with only a sign showed no injury benefit in the one study with a comparison group; red paint has not been tested. New York corners with an object in the space, mostly bike share docks, had about 32% fewer pedestrian injuries than nearby untreated ones. Injuries to everyone, counting drivers and cyclists, did not change significantly.
- **Which way works best?** Something physical where people get hurt most. If posts work as well as New York's mix of objects, they would return about 7 to 96 dollars of injury value per dollar at SF's 250 worst intersections. Nobody has measured posts on their own, and paint's benefit is unmeasured.
- **How would we know?** A dated list of treated corners and comparison corners picked in advance. SF can test its safety zones today.

## San Francisco Now

**SF is painting corners and writing warnings, not tickets, at unpainted ones.** Since 2025-01-01 state law lets a city ticket at an unmarked corner ([Vehicle Code §22500(n)(1)(A), (n)(4)](https://california.public.law/codes/vehicle_code_section_22500)) [secondary — verify]. SFMTA chose warnings until it paints, citing "concerns about fairness in enforcement" ([blog, 2025-02-10](https://www.sfmta.com/blog/making-enforcement-fair-our-new-plan-state-daylighting-law)) [primary — read 2026-10-31]; warnings end at each corner once it is painted, with no citywide end date. Under violation code V22500N ("20FT XWALK"), 13,820 of 13,821 tickets through 2026-10-27 carry a $0 fine, written at 6,373 distinct street addresses; the data records addresses, not corners ([ab4h-6ztd](https://data.sfgov.org/d/ab4h-6ztd)) [DataSF ab4h-6ztd — queried 2026-10-31]. At an unpainted curb nothing marks where 20 feet ends [inference — mine]. Painted corners are ticketed as red zones: 12,916 tickets January to September 2024, 28,542 in the same months of 2026, most in the west, District 7 from 538 to 2,738 [DataSF ab4h-6ztd — queried 2026-10-31].

**How far along is unclear.** SFMTA counted "3,400 of 8,900 intersections citywide", 38% ([Vision Zero Committee deck, 2025-12-04](https://www.sfmta.com/media/44009/download), slide 4) [primary — read 2026-10-31]. For May 2026 it gives two figures: "65% of citywide intersections" on its web page ([SFMTA](https://www.sfmta.com/getting-around/daylighting)) [primary — read 2026-10-31] and 57% in its May 2026 deck (paper trail note, §2) [primary — read 2026-10-07]. That is 1,700 to 2,400 intersections in six months: crews sped up or the count changed [inference — mine]. Its 18 month promise fell due in August 2026.

**SF's one tested result is weak.** SFMTA's Tenderloin sheet reports "14% fewer reported collisions" at 80 intersections, with 180 spaces removed and no comparison ([sheet](https://www.sfmta.com/media/14930/download)) [primary — read 2026-10-31]. Against the rest of the city, people hurt walking there fell 2%, a range of −18% to +17% ([our test](../safe-streets-2026-10/2026-10-21-injury-data-dive.md)) [DataSF nwes-mmgh — queried 2026-10-20]. The Grand Jury says SFMTA found "at least some non-hardened daylighted intersections where collisions have increased" ([report](https://sfgov.legistar.com/View.ashx?M=F&ID=14301843&GUID=A0D9DCE5-FD14-4880-9B9C-889757B3F1D1), p. 26) [Legistar — read 2026-10-31]; it cites that same sheet.

**Hardened corners are slow.** DataSF lists 286 painted safety zones (paint and posts that widen the corner), about 24 a year, 16 in 2025, against SFMTA's target of "approximately 50" ([vtn2-q8ky](https://data.sfgov.org/d/vtn2-q8ky)) [DataSF vtn2-q8ky — queried 2026-10-31]. Posts last about 5 years (SFCTA District 5 request) [primary — read 2026-10-31]. Community Corners, SFMTA's pilot that lets a sponsor adopt a corner with planters or murals, has the sponsor pay to install and keep them up (SFMTA Board Res. 260616-054) [primary — read 2026-10-07].

**Who decides.** Red curb is the City Traffic Engineer's call after a 10 day posted notice, with no hearing; the Board of Supervisors can review a final decision ([SFMTA](https://www.sfmta.com/node/44335)) [primary — read 2026-10-31]. Color curb hearings ended in October 2025 ([notice](https://www.sfmta.com/notices/color-curb-public-hearing-notice-july-26-2024)) [primary — read 2026-10-31]; whether each corner gets its own notice is unconfirmed [unretrieved — verify]. A deadline or quota could come from the SFMTA Board or a Board of Supervisors ordinance [model knowledge — verify]. By ordinance the city may set a different distance with a safety finding, keep commercial loading at named crosswalks, and allow bike parking in the zone; the law names no exception for passenger loading, disabled parking or Muni stops (§22500(n)(1)(B), (n)(2), (n)(3)) [secondary — verify]. Which SF body would adopt such an ordinance wasn't checked [unretrieved — verify]. No ask here needs Sacramento [inference — mine]. Corners on state routes such as 19th Avenue need Caltrans [model knowledge — verify]. Posts and bulb-outs need Fire Department review, with no time limit; Fire "often requests" 26 feet of clear street ([manual](https://www.sfmta.com/media/45260/download?inline), p. 13) [primary — read 2026-10-07]. Fire got Irving Street bulb-outs cut back in 2014 ([Streetsblog SF](https://sf.streetsblog.org/2014/03/12/irving-transit-bulb-outs-downsized-to-appease-sffd-parking-complainers/)) [secondary — verify].

## Equity

**Equity Priority Communities hold 22% of residents and 36% of people hurt walking or biking at intersections:** 154 a year per 100,000 residents against 78 elsewhere, 2021 to 2025, counted where people were hurt ([nwes-mmgh](https://data.sfgov.org/d/nwes-mmgh)) [DataSF nwes-mmgh — queried 2026-10-31]. Half their households have no car (Census survey, [table B25044](https://data.census.gov/table/ACSDT5Y2022.B25044)) [primary — queried 2026-10-31], so most drivers who lose parking likely live elsewhere [inference — mine], though car owners there may have fewer garages.

Red zone fines at ticket locations there rose about $0.6 million a year at face value [inference — mine]. Paint would have to cut injuries at those corners by about 0.4% to be worth that much, and no study shows it cuts them at all. SFMTA painted the 2022 High Injury Network, then blocks near schools, then District 5, chosen because it "has the smallest number of intersections in Phase 3" ([SFCTA request](https://www.sfcta.org/sites/default/files/2025-05/SFCTA_Board_Item6_PropLGroupedAllocationMEMO_2025-05-13.pdf)) [primary — read 2026-10-31]; it names no Equity Priority Community rule. Community Corners costs a $50 fee plus the sponsor's own install and upkeep, with no waiver found, so sponsor corners will likely cluster where residents can pay [inference — mine].

## How to Tell Whether It Helped

**SF can test its painted safety zones now; it can't test red paint, because it publishes no list of painted corners.** The 286 zones see about 90 people hurt walking a year, enough to detect a drop of a third over three years each side, not a smaller one [inference — mine].

A plain before and after credits the fix with citywide trends and the usual easing after a bad stretch; untreated comparison corners, or drawing lots for the order within each priority tier, avoid that [inference — mine].

The scorecard is counts: corners painted out of a published total, corners hardened against 50 a year, and people walking hurt at intersections against about 500 a year in 2021 to 2025 ([nwes-mmgh](https://data.sfgov.org/d/nwes-mmgh)) [DataSF nwes-mmgh — queried 2026-10-31]. A rule could require the corner list, comparison corners named in advance and a yearly analysis.

## What This Means

**The next decision is SFMTA's hardened daylighting plan.** The Street Safety Act urges one for the High Injury Network "by December 2026" ([Res. 437-25](https://sfgov.legistar.com/LegislationDetail.aspx?ID=7501574&GUID=965EDB61-B260-4A6E-A206-99CFF40B0266&FullText=1), §1(d)) [Legistar — read 2026-10-31]. SFMTA told the Grand Jury it began one in July 2025 ([response](https://sfgov.legistar.com/View.ashx?M=F&ID=14749451&GUID=CF2B1EBC-C4DC-4A68-92CD-969CF1A07871), R3.3) [Legistar — read 2026-10-31]; no plan or corner list has been published [unretrieved — verify]. Ask the SFMTA Board, which funds it, to adopt it with a yearly target and a dated public corner list.

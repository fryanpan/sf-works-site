# School Trips: what it takes to get to 30% by car

Working note, 2026-10-19. Bryan asked "how do we actually get to 30% mode share", meaning 30% of SFUSD students arriving by single-family car, with how much money and how fast, grounded in places that made the progress. He added three asks the same day: transit as a lever, a broad survey of other levers, and a palette of what SF could plausibly do in 5 to 10 years. This note builds on what the goals take, the simple track, the city models note, levers not yet in the tool and the car-side pass, and does not repeat them.

The goal is on the model's all-grade base, where 22,184 of 51,769 students (42.9%) go by single-family car. On the Travel Tally's four-grade basis the same model gives 48.6%. The simple-track note uses 51,869 students, 100 more than here, and that difference isn't resolved.

Sources tagged [primary — read 2026-10-19] were opened this session by the research passes that fed this note. Their working files are in the session scratchpad and are not tracked.

## The answer

- **No single package reaches 30% at its central figure, and none on measured evidence. On the Travel Tally's basis, where the goal reads 48% to 30%, even all of them stacked reach only about 33%.** Every SF student count below is a study effect from somewhere else applied to SF, so every one is inferred. The closest, with a driving charge, lands at 30.4% and needs a state law that no California city has won.
- **The levers SF and SFUSD can pull alone get to about 33.8%.** That's the $100M car plan, SFUSD's neighborhood assignment at full effect, the 1,520 yellow bus seats SFUSD has already costed, and more Muni service at the bells. Together they move about 4,700 students out of cars, against the 6,650 the goal needs. They cost about $10M a year on top of the $100M.
- **Adding a circulation plan gets to about 32.3%, and only on an assumption.** In Ghent, escort trips, where someone takes a child somewhere, showed no strong shift. So the measured child effect is zero, and the 780 students counted here have no child basis.
- **Adding a driving charge of London's ULEZ kind gets to about 30.4%, just short.** It's the only lever with a controlled child result of that size. California law bars it today ([CVC §9400.8](https://california.public.law/codes/vehicle_code_section_9400.8)), and SFCTA's downtown pricing study has sat paused since 2022.
- **No school system we found cut its car share by 10 points.** The cuts of 10 points or more are single schools, pilots, or a cross-section against two control schools. The places at or below 30% got there with a large bus or bike share built over decades (Arlington's 44% school bus, Palo Alto's 50% high school bike share), or never left it (Scotland, Japan).

## 3. Costs in SF terms

- **Yellow bus.** SFUSD's costed expansion is "approximately $8.6M ($4.5M increase)" for "19 additional school buses" that "could serve 1520 new students", about $2,960 a rider ([School Access Plan](https://web.archive.org/web/2024id_/https://www.sfcta.org/sites/default/files/2024-07/SF_School_Access_Plan_Report_2023-05-04_FINAL.pdf), p. 41 printed, p. 42 of the PDF [primary — read 2026-10-13]). Its footnote 1 says the $4.5M includes contractual cost growth, so not all of it buys seats. 2023-24 general education service was $6.8M with the state paying 60% ([PPSSF](https://www.ppssf.org/news/2025/2/19/access-at-risk-the-implications-of-cutting-school-buses-in-sfusd) [secondary — verify]). Service continues at 43 schools in 2026-27 ([SFUSD](https://www.sfusd.edu/GEBus) [primary — read 2026-10-19]). No newer cost was found.
- **Muni youth fares.** Free for "all youth 18 years and younger" with no application ([SFMTA](https://www.sfmta.com/fares/free-muni-youth) [primary — read 2026-10-19]). The only cost figure is $2M in the FY2022 budget to offset the expansion ([SFMTA CAC deck](https://sfmta.com/media/28234/download) [primary — read 2026-10-19]). Today's forgone fare is [unretrieved — verify].
- **Muni service.** $301.60 an operating hour for a motor bus ([NTD 2024](https://www.transit.dot.gov/sites/fta.dot.gov/files/transit_agency_profile_doc/2024/90015.pdf) [primary — read 2026-10-13]). The cost note gets about $13 a new trip from that and a service elasticity of 0.5 ([Litman, VTPI 2025](https://www.vtpi.org/tranelas.pdf) [primary — read 2026-10-13]). Litman's 0.5 is for infrequent service, and about 0.3 is typical at 30-minute headways or better, which fits Muni's lines past schools. His "10-50% of the added trips will substitute for an automobile trip" is a fare-cut figure, used here for service because no service figure exists [inference — mine].
- **Crossing guards.** "Roughly $4 million" a year for "about 400 hours of coverage every day" ([SFMTA blog, 2025-08-29](https://www.sfmta.com/blog/prioritizing-safety-schools-citywide-update-our-crossing-guard-program) [primary — read 2026-10-19]), about $56 a guard-hour loaded [inference — mine]. About 165 guards serve 96 schools in 2025-26 ([SFMTA](https://www.sfmta.com/en/node/14991) [primary — read 2026-10-19]).
- **School Streets.** SFMTA publishes no cost. The tool uses $30,000 a school a year plus $17,000 once. Madison's staffing model is about $45,000 a school a year at the guard rate (the car-side note).

## 5. Packages that try to reach 30%

Muni service applies Litman's elasticity to the model's student riders. The circulation plan is points of all students. The charge is a share of the students still driven after everything before it, so no student is counted twice. Low and high run every lever at its own low or high together.

What overlaps, and how we handled it [inference — mine]:

- **Bus and plan.** The plan already funds buses at 2 schools (22 students), so they're subtracted. The bus's 48% previously driven already nets out riders who leave Muni.
- **Bus and assignment.** Assignment cuts TK-5 trips over 2 miles from 28% to between 8% and 24% (assignment options). That lowers demand further, which is one more reason the central stays at the loop take-up rather than every seat filled.
- **Muni and the plan's caregiver fares.** Caregiver fares move parents, and bell-hour service moves the students who ride alone. They're counted separately.
- **Circulation and the plan.** Ghent's 3 points include its own cycle streets, which overlap the plan's bikeways (771 students). The measured child figure is zero, so anything above zero is our assumption.

## 6. On the Travel Tally's basis

The goal is usually stated as "from 48% to 30%", on the SF Travel Tally's four-grade basis. The model puts today at 48.6% on that basis (`school-trips.all.carShareToday` in [`site/data/figures.json`](../../site/data/figures.json)), against 42.9% on all grades. The tally weights K-5 at 60.6%, 6-8 at 15.3% and 9-12 at 24.0% (`tallyBandWeights` in [`site/data/school-sim/city.json`](../../site/data/school-sim/city.json)), and K-5 is the most driven band, at 51.0% by car.

## Gaps

- No cost for Ghent's circulation plan or for an SF equivalent.
- No measured mode effect for a school-hour parking price, a teacher permit cap or a parent-commute policy.
- The share of SF's driven students who live more than 0.86 km from school, where London's effect sits, wasn't computed. It would tighten the charge's range.
- Muni's 25% bell-hour increase is our assumption, and the routes that pass schools weren't mapped. The $13 a trip is an average over all riders, so the Muni cost is a per-trip proxy, not costed bus-hours.
- SFUSD's current yellow bus cost and whether the 2021 Zūm contract was renewed weren't found.
- Paris, Milan, Seville, Utrecht, Boulder and Ontario have no school-trip mode result that we found.

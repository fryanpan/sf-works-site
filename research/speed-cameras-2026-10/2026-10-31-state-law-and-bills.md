# State Law and Bills on Speed Cameras (research note, 2026-10-31)

leginfo.legislature.ca.gov served a bot check to scripts and to Chrome, so the statute and bill text were read from the Legislature's official data file, [pubinfo_2025.zip](https://downloads.leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/).

## 1. What AB 645 Allows SF Today

The law is Vehicle Code §§22425–22431, added by Stats. 2023, ch. 808 (AB 645), effective 2024-01-01, and unamended as of the 2026-10-25 data file [primary — read 2026-10-31].

- **Number of cameras.** A city of 800,000 to 3,000,000 in the 2020 Census gets "no more than 33 systems" ([§22425(b)(2)(B)](https://leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/faces/codes_displaySection.xhtml?lawCode=VEH&sectionNum=22425)) [primary — read 2026-10-31].
- **Eligible streets.** Three kinds: a street meeting the "safety corridor" standard of §22358.7, a street with at least four police calls for speed contests or exhibitions in the prior two years, and school zones, with set hours and flashing beacons (§22425(b)(1)(A)–(C), (c)). No state routes (§22425(e)). Sites must be "geographically and socioeconomically diverse" (§22425(b)(3)) [primary — read 2026-10-31]. How §22358.7 defines a safety corridor [unretrieved — verify]. SFMTA says all 33 SF cameras sit on the 2022 High Injury Network ([18-month report](https://www.sfmta.com/media/50300/download), p. 9) [primary — read 2026-10-31].
- **Fines and thresholds.** Tickets start at 11 mph over (§22426(b)): $50 for 11–15 over, $100 for 16–25, $200 for 26 or more, $500 at 100 mph or more ([§22426(c)](https://leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/faces/codes_displaySection.xhtml?lawCode=VEH&sectionNum=22426)). Civil penalties only, with no points or licence suspension (§22426(a)) [primary — read 2026-10-31].
- **Warnings.** Warnings only for the first 60 days, and for 60 days at each new street. A vehicle's first 11–15 mph violation is always a warning (§22425(f)(2)) [primary — read 2026-10-31].
- **Low-income relief.** Fines are cut 80% for indigent owners and 50% up to 250% of the federal poverty level. Cities must offer community service instead of payment, and payment plans of at most $25 a month with a fee of $5 or less ([§22429(a)–(c)](https://leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/faces/codes_displaySection.xhtml?lawCode=VEH&sectionNum=22429)) [primary — read 2026-10-31].
- **Revenue and maintenance of effort.** Revenue first covers program costs. To stay in the pilot a city must keep spending at least its FY2016-17 to 2018-19 average on traffic calming, excluding bond and tax-measure money. Surplus goes to traffic calming within three years, or else to the state Active Transportation Program (§22426(g)) [primary — read 2026-10-31].
- **Data limits.** Records are confidential and usable only for the program or to assess it (§22425(l)(1)). Photos that don't produce a ticket are destroyed within five business days, and facial recognition is banned ((l)(4)). Data may not go to "any other state or federal government agency or official for any other purpose", except as state or federal law, a court order or a subpoena in an individual case requires ((l)(5)) [primary — read 2026-10-31]. Immigration isn't named; the "federal law" exception is what an opponent will point to [inference — mine].
- **The 18-month test.** Each camera must cut the 85th-percentile speed, or cut drivers 10+ mph over by 20%, or cut repeat violators by 20%, or else come down unless the city adds traffic calming (§22425(p)) [primary — read 2026-10-31].
- **Reporting.** By March 1 of "the fifth year in which the system has been implemented", each city reports to the Legislature's transportation committees: speed shares before and after, notices by month and place, repeat violators, crashes at sites against the citywide trend by mode and severity, payment, delinquency and dismissals at each review level, costs and revenue, and notices to indigent owners, to owners up to 250% of poverty, and by ZIP code ([§22430(a)–(f)](https://leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/faces/codes_displaySection.xhtml?lawCode=VEH&sectionNum=22430)) [primary — read 2026-10-31]. For SF that is probably 2029-03-01 [inference — mine].
- **Sunset.** The article is repealed 2032-01-01 ([§22431](https://leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/faces/codes_displaySection.xhtml?lawCode=VEH&sectionNum=22431)). Separately, "A speed safety system may only be in operation for five years, or until January 1, 2032, whichever date is sooner" (§22426(i)) [primary — read 2026-10-31]. If the five years run from SF's March 2025 launch, SF's cameras stop around March 2030 [inference — mine; ask SFMTA or the City Attorney how they read it].

## 2. Bills in 2025–26 and the Board's Resolution

A title search of every 2025–26 bill in the data file found these [primary — read 2026-10-31]:

- **[SB 1279 (Gonzalez)](https://leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/faces/billNavClient.xhtml?bill_id=202520260SB1279), Chapter 689 of 2026, signed 2026-09-27, in effect 2027-01-01.** Two more Long Beach cameras on Pacific Coast Highway, a state route. For every city, cameras must blur drivers, vehicles and pedestrians who aren't the subject of a ticket; cameras installed before 2027 only "to the extent feasible" (amended §22425(b)(3), (e)(2), (q)(2)–(3)). Assembly amendments cut the ask from five cameras to two and folded it into AB 645 ([Senate floor analysis, 8/24/26](https://leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/faces/billAnalysisClient.xhtml?bill_id=202520260SB1279)). The Assembly Transportation analysis said: "The Legislature should wait for data to come back on the existing pilot programs before expanding." Support included AAA Northern California, California Bicycle Coalition and Walk San Francisco Foundation. The sole opponent, the National Motorists Association, said AB 645's negotiated limits "must be honored". Votes: 33–6 Senate, 65–7 Assembly [primary — read 2026-10-31].
- **[AB 1379 (Nguyen)](https://leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/faces/billNavClient.xhtml?bill_id=202520260AB1379)** would have added Sacramento. Never heard; died 2026-01-31 [primary — read 2026-10-31].
- Separate pilots: Caltrans work zones ([AB 289](https://leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/faces/billNavClient.xhtml?bill_id=202520260AB289), 2025) and Malibu's PCH (SB 1297, 2024).

No 2025–26 bill raised SF's 33, added mobile cameras or extended 2032 [inference — mine, from titles; a change to §22425 under an unrelated title would be missed]. Mobile units are already allowed two years after the first fixed camera, or sooner if kept at one spot (§22426(f)) [primary — read 2026-10-31].

**Board of Supervisors [File 260499](https://sfgov.legistar.com/LegislationDetail.aspx?ID=8008866&GUID=B1FBD6C3-549E-4BE9-A81A-B01ACEA90488), Resolution 408-26.** Lead sponsor Dorsey; adopted 10–1 on 2026-07-14 (Walton no); returned unsigned by the Mayor 2026-07-24. The operative clause only "supports exploring changes to State law ... including increasing the number of allowable speed camera locations, with priority given to corridors on the High Injury Network." The "at least 80 additional locations" is in a WHEREAS clause, not the RESOLVED clause ([final text](https://sfgov.legistar.com/View.ashx?M=F&ID=15722015&GUID=42B512F2-5591-41C4-8F61-337C18841601), p. 2) [Legistar — read 2026-10-31]. SFMTA "expects to advocate for expansion of the program to incrementally increase the number of camera locations, extension of the program, and refinements" ([18-month report](https://www.sfmta.com/media/50300/download), p. 4) [primary — read 2026-10-31].

## 4. The 2027 Calendar

The 2027 deadlines aren't published [unretrieved — verify]. The [2026 calendar](https://www.assembly.ca.gov/media/2331) shows the pattern [primary — read 2026-10-31]:

- **2026-12-07:** the 2027–28 session convenes.
- **Late January:** bill requests due at Legislative Counsel (2026-01-23).
- **Third Friday of February:** last day to introduce (2026-02-20, when SB 1279 went in; AB 1379 went in 2025-02-21).
- **30 days in print** before a hearing (AB 1379: "May be heard in committee March 24").
- **Late April to late May:** first-house committee and floor deadlines (2026-04-24, 2026-05-29).
- **End of session:** last votes 2026-08-31; Governor's deadline 2026-09-30; bills take effect January 1. Odd-year end dates differ [unretrieved — verify].

A bill introduced in 2027 that stalls can still pass its first house by 2028-01-31, the deadline the calendar sets for odd-year bills [primary — read 2026-10-31].

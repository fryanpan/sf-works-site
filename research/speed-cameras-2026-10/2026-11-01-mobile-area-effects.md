# Area-wide effects of mobile and rotating speed cameras

Research note on rotating cameras, checked 2026-11-01. Queensland's 2 km result and the UK site results are in 2026-11-01-school-zone-and-mobile-evidence.md.

## Victoria: covert, unsigned mobile cameras

- **1990–91, network-wide: casualty crashes down 32% on Melbourne arterials, 23% in country towns and 14% on rural highways.** Low-alcohol (mostly daytime) hours from July 1990, against trend and comparable New South Wales areas ([Cameron et al. 1992](https://www.monash.edu/muarc/archive/our-publications/reports/muarc042), via [Cameron and Delaney 2007](https://www.monash.edu/muarc/archive/our-publications/reports/muarc270), p. 5) [primary — read 2026-11-01]. The 1992 scan was unreadable.
- **Near sites, the effect was small and came on top of the network effect.** Across 14,504 casualty crashes within 1 km of 1,699 sites, crashes fell only in "high alcohol hours" on arterials after a ticket arrived. There was no fall in the week after a session ([MUARC 54](https://www.monash.edu/muarc/archive/our-publications/reports/muarc054)) [primary — read 2026-11-01]. MUARC's review calls this "a general effect much broader than any localised effects". The early overt program "could not be shown to reduce casualty crash frequency" ([MUARC 200](https://www.monash.edu/muarc/archive/our-publications/reports/muarc200)) [primary — read 2026-11-01].
- **2000–02: 50% more camera hours, 3.25% fewer casualty crashes statewide.** Each 1% rise in hours brought a 0.09% fall, by police region, 1998–2003 ([Bobevski et al. 2004, via MUARC 270](https://www.monash.edu/muarc/archive/our-publications/reports/muarc270), p. 5) [primary — read 2026-11-01].

## Mobile against fixed, area-wide

**No study compares the two head to head.** Cameron and Delaney's Table 2 ([p. 22](https://www.monash.edu/muarc/archive/our-publications/reports/muarc270)) [primary — read 2026-11-01] sets separate evaluations side by side:

| Program | At sites | Area-wide |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Great Britain, fixed / mobile | serious casualty crashes −65% / −28% | not measured |
| New Zealand, overt mobile | serious casualty crashes −23% | −13% |
| Queensland, randomly scheduled mobile | casualty crashes −35% | −26% |
| Victoria, covert mobile | not given | casualty crashes −21% (Melbourne −32%) |

Designs, years and denominators differ; the rows rank nothing.

- **Høye's meta-analysis of fixed cameras:** about 20% fewer crashes, and "The effect declines with increasing distance from the camera location." No area-wide mobile estimate ([2014](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/25247551/)) [primary — read 2026-11-01, abstract].
- **Norway, fixed cameras:** 20% fewer injury crashes on 64 sections covering 336 km. Nothing beyond the sections was measured ([Elvik 1997, via MUARC 270](https://www.monash.edu/muarc/archive/our-publications/reports/muarc270), p. 16) [primary — read 2026-11-01]. We didn't reach Elvik's handbook or any Swedish study [unretrieved — verify].

## PA/UCL 2004: nothing beyond the sites

Every estimate counts crashes at camera sites, against a trend in areas not yet in the program (Appendix G.3). A mobile site is 400 to 3,000 m long (Table 22). No route or area effect is estimated. Its overview says the benefits "had been extended to a much wider area" (p. 7), and no analysis supports that line ([PA Consulting and UCL](https://discovery.ucl.ac.uk/id/eprint/1338/1/2004_31.pdf)) [primary — read 2026-11-01]. Cameron and Delaney note that a halo seen in the UK pilots "has not been identified in the later evaluations" (p. 16) [primary — read 2026-11-01].

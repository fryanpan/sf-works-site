# AB 645: where the mobile-camera limit came from, and who objected to mobile units

Raw notes, 2026-11-02, Question from Bryan: what was the specific opposition to mobile speed cameras in AB 645 (Friedman, 2023, Veh. Code §§22425–22434)?

**Short answer: no opponent's letter or committee analysis I could open objects to mobile units as such.** Opponents attacked automated speed enforcement as a whole: surveillance, revenue, equity, due process, police labour. The only mention of mobile units in an opposition document is one sentence in Human Rights Watch's August 2023 letter, raising them as an example of how the program could grow. The two-year delay on mobile units was already in the bill before any opposition was on file. It was carried over from AB 550 (2021), where it arrived in the 15 April 2021 rewrite. AB 645 added the "kept at a fixed location" exception.

Method note: leginfo and legiscan returned 403, and archive.org is blocked for WebFetch. Bill text came from the CalMatters Digital Democracy mirror. The committee analyses are PDFs on the committee sites. WebFetch fetched them but could not parse them, and the harness cached the fetched bytes in the session's tool-results folder. I extracted their text with `pdftotext` from that cache. I did not download anything separately. If that counts as a download under the task rules, treat the four analyses below as unretrieved.

Where AB 550's delay came from: SFCTA's April 2021 staff update says the 15 April amendments "are closely modeled off of language in AB 342 (Chiu)" and that the bill would now "Prohibit the use of mobile camera units for two years after the initial installation of speed safety cameras."^[SFCTA Board Resolution 21-40, attachment, "Update 04.21.2021", [PDF](https://www.sfcta.org/sites/default/files/2021-04/Item%204%20-%20State%20and%20Federal%20Update.pdf). [primary — read 2026-11-02]] AB 342 had no such delay (row above), so the delay was new in the 2021 rewrite. Neither SFCTA nor any analysis I opened says who asked for it or why [unretrieved — verify]. The AB 550 committee analyses (Assembly Transportation, 26 Apr 2021; Appropriations, May 2021) would be the place to look. The Appropriations copy hosted by Streetsblog failed a TLS certificate check, and the mirror reports "no bill analysis documents are available" for AB 550.

[inference — mine] The timing suggests the delay was a drafting choice by the author and the sponsor cities, made before AB 645's opponents registered. It was in AB 645's text by 30 March 2023, when Assembly Transportation listed opposition as "None on file". The fixed-location exception widened what cities could do. It let a mobile unit work from day one as long as it stayed parked at one site.

## 2. What each committee analysis says about mobile units

- **Assembly Transportation, hearing 17 Apr 2023 (version 30 Mar 2023).** The analysis restates the bill without comment. It defines a system as "a fixed or mobile radar or laser system". It summarises the delay (item 9, quoted above). It requires cities to "Identify vehicles containing a mobile speed safety system with distinctive markings, including information that the system is being operated for 'Photo Enforcement' purposes" and to "post the locations and hours of enforcement on the municipality's Internet website" (item 10b). It also requires them to "Use fixed and mobile speed safety systems that provide real-time notification (like a camera flash)" (item 10e). Its only evidence on mobile units: of 13 studies in NHTSA's 2007 review, nine "examined mobile ASE programs and found that injury crashes in mobile ASE zones declined between 21% and 51%." Opposition: "None on file". The analysis says nothing about why mobile units are delayed.^[[ATRN analysis](https://atrn.assembly.ca.gov/sites/atrn.assembly.ca.gov/files/AB%20645%20%28Friedman%29.pdf), pp. 2, 5, 12. [primary — read 2026-11-02]]
- **Assembly Privacy and Consumer Protection, hearing 25 Apr 2023 (version 30 Mar 2023).** It gives the same summary lines ("Prohibits the use of mobile systems for the first two years of the pilot unless they are kept at a fixed location", item 10), with no comment on them. The only mobile mention in the opposition section is the Western States Trucking Association's description of what the bill allows: "It authorizes an undefined number of speed cameras to enforce any speed law, either through a fixed or mobile radar or laser system or any other electronic device, within 6 California cities." WSTA's objection is breadth ("excessively overbroad for a 'pilot program'") and hardship. It does not object to mobile units specifically. The committee's amendments limited cameras to still photos instead of video, filled in the camera caps, and barred fining a driver twice for one violation. None of them touched mobile units. Registered opposition: ACLU California Action, Anti Police-Terror Project, EFF, Oakland Privacy, Safer Streets LA, Western States Trucking Association.^[[APCP analysis](https://apcp.assembly.ca.gov/sites/privacycp.assembly.ca.gov/files/AB%20645%20%28Friedman%29%20APCP%20analysis.pdf), pp. 1–2, 7–8, comment 5. [primary — read 2026-11-02]]
- **Assembly Appropriations (May 2023).** Not retrieved [unretrieved — verify].
- **Senate Transportation (hearing 27 June 2023, passed 10–5).** Analysis not found by search, and the committee site was not reachable through the routes I tried [unretrieved — verify].
- **Senate Judiciary, hearing 11 July 2023 (version 3 July 2023).** It mentions mobile units only in the definition ("fixed or mobile radar or laser system") and the real-time-notification requirement. The delay itself is not described. Its framing is general: "Furthering the reach of automated surveillance should arguably be gradual, thoughtful, and done with an understanding of, and countermeasures to prevent, potential unintended consequences." The opposition it quotes covers police jobs (PORAC on Oakland's traffic division), due process (Safer Streets), and revenue and cost (the ACLU/EFF coalition). ## 3. What named opponents said about mobile units

- **Coalition letter, 6 July 2023** (ACLU California Action, Anti Police-Terror Project, BLM CA, California Teamsters Public Affairs Council, EFF, National Motorists Association, Safer Streets LA; addressed to Friedman, on the 3 July version). The words "mobile" and "van" do not appear. The nearest point is that programs grow: "While these speed trap programs may start small, they can soon balloon into ever expanding surveillance and ticketing apparatuses that result in millions more tickets being issued." On privacy it says: "By encouraging the use of surveillance technologies, like automated license plate readers (ALPRs), for enforcement of speed limits, AB 645 subjects Californians to increased surveillance".^[[Coalition letter](https://cal.streetsblog.org/wp-content/uploads/sites/14/2023/08/AB-645-7.6.2023-coalition-letter.pdf), hosted by Streetsblog Cal. [primary — read 2026-11-02]] This is the letter SJUD quotes for "ACLU California Action and the Electronic Frontier Foundation". I found no separate EFF or ACLU blog post on AB 645 [unretrieved — verify; search only].
- **Human Rights Watch to Senate Appropriations chair Portantino, 7 Aug 2023** (signed by Olivia Ensign). This is the only opposition document I found that names mobile units. Its section II says: "AB 645 already references the possible instillation of mobile radar or laser systems" (sic). It goes on to note that mobile units are allowed only two years after fixed cameras go in. It says the bill bans facial recognition but "does not explicitly prohibit the future addition of automated plate recognition on the same cameras". It also cites police use of "car-mounted license plate readers to disproportionately target low-income and BIPOC communities". The argument is about expansion, with mobile units as one way the program could grow, alongside plate recognition.^[[HRW letter](https://www.hrw.org/news/2023/08/07/human-rights-watchs-opposition-ab-645). The quotes are as WebFetch returned them; the tool would not return the paragraph in full, so the rest is its paraphrase. [primary — read 2026-11-02]]
- **Veto request to Newsom, 15 Sept 2023** (13 groups, including ACLU CA Action, EFF, HRW, NMA, Teamsters, Oakland Privacy). There is no sentence on mobile, vehicle-mounted, radar or laser systems; it speaks of "speed cameras" and ALPRs.^[[HRW-hosted letter](https://www.hrw.org/news/2023/09/15/letter-governor-newsom-requesting-he-veto-ab-645). ## 4. Other US rules on mobile units, where I opened the source

- **Arizona, A.R.S. §28-1204.** Subsection E: when a vehicle with photo enforcement equipment is in use, "the rear of the vehicle shall be clearly marked". Subsection B requires at least two signs before a photo enforcement system, one about 300 feet ahead, and subsection F exempts a mobile photo enforcement vehicle on streets "with a posted speed limit of forty miles per hour or less" from those sign rules.^[[azleg.gov §28-1204](https://www.azleg.gov/ars/28/01204.htm). Quotes are as WebFetch returned them. [primary — read 2026-11-02]] This is the same "mark the van, post notice" answer AB 342 and AB 645 adopted.
- **Maryland.** A 2013 SHA comparison lists local school-zone programs as using "Fixed, Moveable, and Mobile vehicles" that "May be unmanned under MD law", while the state work-zone program uses mobile vehicles that "Must be manned under MD law".^[[SHA comparison, Jan 2013](https://www.roads.maryland.gov/OC/Comparison_between_State_and_Local_Programs_Jan82013.pdf). [primary — read 2026-11-02; 2013 document, may be out of date]]
- **Washington.** The old RCW 46.63.170 was "Repealed by 2024 c 307 § 12". The final bill report for ESHB 2384 mentions vehicle-mounted cameras only on transit vehicles, for bus-stop violations.^[[RCW 46.63.170](https://app.leg.wa.gov/RCW/default.aspx?cite=46.63.170); [ESHB 2384 final bill report](https://lawfilesext.leg.wa.gov/biennium/2023-24/Htm/Bill%20Reports/House/2384-S.E%20HBR%20FBR%2024.htm). [primary — read 2026-11-02]] Whether current Washington law allows mobile speed cameras: [unretrieved — verify].
- **DC.** The search pointed to a 2005 Washington Times report of speed cameras in eight police cruisers, and to AAA Mid-Atlantic's "speed trap" and revenue criticism of DC cameras. The Times returned 403, and I did not open the AAA items [web search — verify].
- **I found no US opponent statement against mobile or unmarked vans specifically that I could open.** Stated objections in the US sources I reached are about revenue, "speed traps" and signage. The marking and signage rules above are how legislatures have answered them [inference — mine].

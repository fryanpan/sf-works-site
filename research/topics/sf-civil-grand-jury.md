# San Francisco Civil Grand Jury reports, 2021-22 to 2025-26

The Civil Grand Jury is 19 residents who serve for a year, July through June, investigating the city's own departments.^[sf.gov, [Civil Grand Jury department page](https://www.sf.gov/departments--civil-grand-jury). [primary — read 2026-10-22]] Each report below answers four questions: what the jury found broken, what it asked for, how the city replied, and whether anything has since been done. R1.1 and the like are the jury's recommendation numbers, and the four kinds of answer the city may give are explained at the end.

*Done* in a count means the city said so. It does not mean anyone checked. ### [At Scale, At Risk: Upgrading Data and Oversight to Improve Homelessness Services](https://api.sf.gov/documents/59416/2026_CGJ_Rpt_At_Scale_At_Risk_-_Upgrading_Data_and_Oversight_to_Improve_Homele_woZ0ksh.pdf), 2026-06-23

**Gaps found**^[2025-26 Civil Grand Jury, *At Scale, At Risk*, Findings F1 to F5, [archived text](../../data/cgj/text/2026-at-scale-at-risk.txt). [primary — read 2026-10-22]]

- The homelessness department (HSH) collects thousands of critical incident reports a year but does not analyze them across the system. On a 2024 Controller recommendation to do so, the jury's "research did not reveal that any steps had been taken toward implementation".
- Permanent supportive housing sites accounted for about 26% "of all accidental drug overdose deaths in San Francisco" in 2024.
- Since May 2025, HSH has stopped bringing contract renewals to the Homelessness Oversight Commission (HOC). They "will no longer be presented to HOC for public review", although renewals are most of HSH's contracting.
- The Homelessness Oversight Commission has never used its Charter power to audit HSH. Once the Shelter Monitoring Committee ends, "there will be no independent body authorized to conduct unannounced inspections".

**Key recommendations**

- R1.3: By June 2027, publish anonymized incident data as a public dashboard.
- R1.4: By December 2027, write safety standards into every nonprofit contract, with thresholds that trigger review.
- R3.1: By December 2026, put contract renewals back before the Commission.
- R4.3: By March 2027, the Commission should audit overdoses and deaths at supportive housing sites.

Homelessness Oversight Commission, [response](https://api.sf.gov/documents/63739/At_Scale_At_Risk_Report_Response_HOC_091426_Updated_092226.pdf), 2026-09-22. [primary — read 2026-10-22]]

- Refused: the public incident dashboard (R1.3), Commission review of renewals (R3.1 and R3.2), the overdose audit (R4.3), and the Controller reviews of shelter monitoring (R5.1 and R5.2).
- The Commission disagreed with the Mayor on five. It sent R1.3, R1.4, R2.1, R3.1 and R4.3 to further analysis, and said it will adopt R3.2.
- The Board agreed with F2, partly agreed with F1 and partly disagreed with F3, and of the four recommendations put to it reported R1.1 and R2.1 done and refused R1.4, which it left to HSH. It also refused R3.1, saying renewals on the consent calendar "would add an administrative step" without materially helping oversight.^[Board of Supervisors, Resolution 555-26, [File 260730](https://sfgov.legistar.com/LegislationDetail.aspx?ID=8118902&GUID=E895D4E1-EDA7-4A8A-A428-666F4C970D3F), adopted 2026-09-29, [response](https://api.sf.gov/documents/65078/At_Scale_At_Risk_Report_Response_BOS_102826.pdf) posted 2026-10-28. ### [Fog of Warning: San Francisco's Warning System That Warns but Doesn't Inform](https://api.sf.gov/documents/59312/2026CG1_XrFf8C0.PDF), 2026-06-18

**Gaps found**^[2025-26 Civil Grand Jury, *Fog of Warning*, Findings, [archived text](../../data/cgj/text/2026-fog-of-warning.txt). [primary — read 2026-10-22]]

- City alerts rarely say who is sending, what the hazard is, where, what to do and for how long. "Not a single message in the 329-message archive contained all five elements at full presence."
- The Department of Emergency Management (DEM) has written rules for Wireless Emergency Alerts (WEA), the messages sent to every phone in an area, for few incidents. "DEM's protocols specify WEA as a channel for only two of 38 incident types".
- No incident type has a closure procedure telling people the danger has passed.
- AlertSF, the city's sign-up text alert service, reaches a small share of the city, and its promotion costs "an estimated $20,000 to $30,000 annually with no dedicated paid media".

**Key recommendations**

- R1: By 2028-01-01, DEM should adopt one messaging standard requiring all five elements on every channel.
- R2: By 2028-01-01, adopt written criteria for when to send a Wireless Emergency Alert, for all 38 incident types.
- R4 and R6: Write an AlertSF growth strategy with enrollment targets, and by 2028-07-01 the Mayor and Board should decide whether to fund it.

**Response**^[Mayor, DEM, Police and Fire, [consolidated response](https://api.sf.gov/documents/62501/Fog_of_Warning_Report_Responses_MYR_081726_consolidated_hjp8kt9.pdf), 2026-08-17. [primary — read 2026-10-22]]

- The Mayor answered for DEM, Police and Fire. All 7 recommendations are done or will be, 3 of them reported done, and none was refused. `[inference — mine]`
- Two of the three marked done describe future work. R6 is left to the budget process, and R7's web destination is due by 2028-01-01.
- The Board agreed with all three findings put to it (F14 to F16), adding that the department's leadership "has also not prioritized audience growth". It refused R6, the AlertSF funding decision, as "not warranted or reasonable" and left it to the annual budget process.^[Board of Supervisors, Resolution 554-26, [File 260722](https://sfgov.legistar.com/LegislationDetail.aspx?ID=8092065&GUID=20A8BCEF-0D02-411F-BC68-6CE04AC31AB8), adopted 2026-09-29, [response](https://api.sf.gov/documents/65077/Fog_of_Warning_Report_Response_BOS_102826.pdf) posted 2026-10-28. [primary — read 2026-11-19]]

**Followed through?** Unknown: no follow-up found. The promised dates run to 2028-01-01. The DEM's published messaging standard and alert criteria, once due, would settle it.

### [The Sounds of Silence: San Francisco's Outdoor Public Warning System and the Cost of Not Deciding](https://api.sf.gov/documents/58919/2026CG1.PDF), 2026-06-16

**Gaps found**^[2025-26 Civil Grand Jury, *The Sounds of Silence*, Findings and Analysis, [archived text](../../data/cgj/text/2026-sounds-of-silence.txt). [primary — read 2026-10-22]]

- "The siren system has been inoperable since 2019 due to security vulnerabilities and deteriorating physical infrastructure without a firm commitment of resources for its restoration."
- No one has decided to restore the sirens or retire them, and the open question costs Emergency Management (DEM) staff time.
- Public information about the sirens "is outdated and contradictory". The August 2023 announcement of their return is still online.
- DEM estimated in December 2024 that full restoration would cost about $20.8 million, or $8.5 million for tsunami zones only. Three capital requests were denied.

**Key recommendations**

- R1: By 2027-07-01, DEM should formally advise the Mayor and Board whether to restore the sirens or retire them for good.
- R2: By 2028-01-01, remove or secure leftover siren hardware that is a hazard.
- R4: By 2027-04-01, publish a plain explanation of the city's alerting strategy and correct the siren pages.

**Response**^[Mayor, DEM and Public Works, [consolidated response](https://api.sf.gov/documents/62491/The_Sounds_of_Silence_Report_Responses_MYR_081726_consolidated.pdf), 2026-08-17. [primary — read 2026-10-22]]

- The Mayor answered for DEM and Public Works. Of 4 recommendations: 2 done or will be, 2 will not be implemented. `[inference — mine]`
- Refused: R1, the restore or retire decision itself. The city says "This decision must be made in coordination with the City's capital planning process and elected and department leadership."
- DEM also refused R3, a preserved siren as a monument.
- R2 is marked done, but its text says removing all the sirens is expected to take about two more years.
- Of six findings put to it, the Board agreed with four (F7 and F10 to F12), partly agreed with F9 and partly disagreed with F6. It refused R1 as "not warranted or reasonable", saying the decision "needs to be made in conjunction with elected and department leadership", and reported R2 "has already been implemented".^[Board of Supervisors, Resolution 553-26, [File 260704](https://sfgov.legistar.com/LegislationDetail.aspx?ID=8073916&GUID=8B340C81-F586-42BC-BBD1-7D245EAD4F19), adopted 2026-09-29, [response](https://api.sf.gov/documents/65076/The_Sounds_of_Silence_Report_Response_BOS_102826.pdf) posted 2026-10-28. [primary — read 2026-11-19]]

**Followed through?** Unknown: no follow-up found. Before the report, the Board had already urged restoration in "Resolution No. 130-26, File No. 260142".^[2025-26 Civil Grand Jury, *The Sounds of Silence*, [archived text](../../data/cgj/text/2026-sounds-of-silence.txt). [primary — read 2026-10-22]] A decision by the Mayor or the Capital Planning Committee would settle it.

### [When Making Do Doesn't Work: San Francisco Jails in Crisis](https://api.sf.gov/documents/57224/2026_CGJ_Report_When_Making_Do_Doesnt_Work_-_San_Francisco_Jails_in_Crisis.pdf), 2026-06-09

- R12: By 2026-11-01, the Controller should audit the Sheriff's staffing and overtime.

### [Red Flag Warning: Wildfire Risk in San Francisco's Glen Canyon](https://api.sf.gov/documents/57022/2026_CGJ_Report_Red_Flag_Warning_-_Wildfire_Risk_in_San_Franciscos_Glen_Canyon.pdf), 2026-06-04

- "City agencies with wildland properties generally lack current Wildfire Mitigation Plans."

- R4.2: By 2027-06-30, each land-holding department should write a wildfire mitigation plan and update it yearly.

- R4.3: By 2026-03-31, SFMTA should define and publish yearly measures of both crashes and how safe people feel on the street, for example from surveys.
- R5.1: By 2026-03-31, SFMTA should run a new public campaign teaching all road users to avoid the violations behind the worst crashes.

### [Buried Problems and a Buried Process: The Hunters Point Naval Shipyard in a Time of Climate Change](https://civilgrandjury.archive.sf.gov/2021_2022/2022%20CGJ%20Report_Buried%20Problems%20and%20a%20Buried%20Process%20-%20The%20Hunters%20Point%20Naval%20Shipyard%20in%20a%20Time%20of%20Climate%20Change.pdf), 2022-06-14

**Gaps found**^[2021-22 Civil Grand Jury, *Buried Problems and a Buried Process*, Findings F1 to F6, [archived text](../../data/cgj/text/2022-hunters-point-shipyard.txt). [primary — read 2026-10-22]]

- "shallow groundwater rising with sea level rise and residual hazardous substances pose serious but poorly understood risks" at the Shipyard.
- The Navy and its regulators "have neglected to investigate how groundwater rise may lessen the effectiveness of the Navy's cleanup".
- The city's Superfund expertise sits in one Public Health program "that until recently had only one employee".
- "No proactive mechanism exists" for the city to raise concerns about the cleanup and track the answers.

**Key recommendations**

- R1: By 2022-09-01, commission an independent study of future groundwater levels at the Shipyard.

- R3: By 2022-10-01, create a permanent cleanup oversight committee by ordinance.

**Response**^[Mayor, [response](https://civilgrandjury.archive.sf.gov/2021_2022/Hunters%20Point%20Response_MYR_081122.pdf), 2022-08-11. Public Health, [response](https://civilgrandjury.archive.sf.gov/2021_2022/Hunters%20Point%20Report%20Response_DPH_091522.pdf), 2022-09-15. Board of Supervisors, [Resolution 437-22](https://sfbos.org/sites/default/files/r0437-22.pdf), [File 220721](https://sfgov.legistar.com/LegislationDetail.aspx?ID=5724713&GUID=EC939D42-15B6-4361-853A-04229CF21C4F), passed 2022-10-18. ### [Shovel Ready: Best Practices and Collaboration to Improve San Francisco's Capital Construction Program](https://civilgrandjury.archive.sf.gov/2021_2022/2022%20CGJ%20Report_Shovel%20Ready%20-%20Best%20Practices%20and%20Collaboration%20to%20Improve%20San%20Francisco's%20Capital%20Construction%20Program.pdf), 2022-04-11

## How reports get answered

- The Mayor has 60 days to answer and the Board of Supervisors 90.^[2024-25 Civil Grand Jury, [Failed Vision](https://api.sf.gov/documents/41530/2025_CGJ_Report_Failed_Vision.pdf), p. 38: "within 60 calendar days (from the Mayor) and 90 calendar days (from the Board of Supervisors)." [primary — read 2026-10-22]] Department heads are asked to answer within 60 days.
- Each answer agrees or disagrees with each finding. It then says each recommendation has been implemented, will be implemented, requires further analysis, or will not be implemented.^[The responses archived in [`data/cgj/reports.json`](../../data/cgj/reports.json), each citing Penal Code 933 and 933.05. [primary — read 2026-10-22]]
- The counts on this page take one answer per recommendation, from the city's response, or from the Board where only the Board was asked. The Board's own answers are listed separately.
- Where respondents differ, both are named.
- In most terms the Mayor's office files one consolidated response for itself and the departments.
- The Board answers by resolution. Its Government Audit and Oversight Committee holds a hearing and sends a draft to the full Board, citing Penal Code Section 933 for a duty to respond "within 90 days of receipt".^[Board of Supervisors, [Resolution 455-25](https://sfbos.org/sites/default/files/r0455-25.pdf), File 250668, WHEREAS clauses. [primary — read 2026-10-22]]
- Of the 12 Board resolutions for 2021-22 to 2024-25, 11 passed more than 90 days after the report's release. The clock runs from the Board's receipt, which we have not found, so whether any was late is open. `[inference — mine]`
- The Controller follows up each term's recommendations a year or more later and posts the status at [sf.gov](https://www.sf.gov/information--status-civil-grand-jury-recommendations).

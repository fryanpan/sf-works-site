It covers who decides each step, the law, what SF has built and its own reports, the totals we reproduced and the ones we didn't, SF's own before and after numbers, and the test we could run. ## Short answer

- **SF has built few traffic circles and almost none recently.** DataSF lists 35 traffic circles, the newest two from January 2020 on the 20th Avenue Neighborway; 13 carry no date and 12 of those 13 are in Parkmerced. Circles in SF are a horizontal calming device inside the old Residential Traffic Calming Program, not a School Connector tool yet.
- **SF's diverters are Slow Street diverters.** In law they are turn restrictions "EXCEPT BICYCLES" plus posts or a concrete island, legislated one intersection at a time. Shotwell, Page, Minnesota, Cayuga and Golden Gate Avenue have them. Shotwell (median diverters, next to Cesar Chavez Elementary) met both Board targets in 2023; Page did not; Cayuga fell from about 2,100 to 1,200 cars a day after its Geneva diverter, by SFMTA's account.
- **Who decides: the City Traffic Engineer, at the weekly hearing, for almost all of it.** SFMTA staff proposed in 2022 that every "traffic diversion" need an SFMTA Board vote. The Board struck that clause on 2022-12-06 before adopting the Slow Streets Program. Adding a street to the Slow Streets Program, and blocking entry under Vehicle Code §21101(g) as on Page at Divisadero, go to the SFMTA Board. Other repo notes still carry the staff proposal as if adopted; see "Conflicts" below.
- **Fire review is settled on paper for soft diverters and mountable circles.** The 2026 SFMTA and SFFD manual gives soft diversion and circles with a 2 inch mountable apron limited review, and hard diversion extended review. The same manual's own matrix puts all traffic circles in the one-month tier, so the manual disagrees with itself on circles.
- **SF's own crash record on Slow Streets, rerun with today's data, shows a real drop.** Injury crashes on 16 Slow Street corridors fell 41% from 2017 to 2019 against 2023 to 2025, while the city fell 13%; the ratio is 0.68 (95% interval about 0.52 to 0.88). SFMTA's own 48% figure used 2022 crash counts that DataSF now shows 70% higher. Nobody has counted crashes on the streets that took the diverted traffic.

**Diverters on Slow Streets.** The 2023 toolkit defines four volume tools [primary — read 2026-10-31]:
- "Soft Diversion - Traffic Diverter and Sign at Intersections" "discourages cut-through traffic while still allowing for local access". "Traffic diverters cannot be installed at intersections where a traffic signal is present without an associated left-turn restriction on the cross street" (PDF p. 1).
- "Left-Turn Restrictions": "Local traffic, such as residents and mail/ delivery vehicles, can only access the block by making a right turn onto it."
- "Median Diverters": "created by installing several traffic delineators in the middle of the intersection", "fully preventing a vehicle from continuing through to the next block and forcing vehicles to turn right".
- "Concrete Islands": "a more durable barrier", "while allowing for bicycle and scooter access".
- And under speed tools, "Neighborhood Traffic Circle": "usually built of concrete, but other materials like safe-hit posts and paint can be used. The element in the middle of the intersection prevents drivers from traveling straight through and slows down vehicles" (PDF p. 2).

**The struck clause.** The staff report proposed a new §201(c)(18): Board approval for "Traffic diversions, defined as two or more turn restrictions at an intersection approach accompanied by physical measures to compel compliance other than standard signs and markings", "on all streets in the City network" (PDF pp. 7, 10, 19) [primary — read 2026-10-31]. The Board's motion was "to remove Section 201(c)(18) and strike the addition to Section 201(b)(7)" and to delete "and both in the context of the program and otherwise to implement certain traffic diversions" from the first Resolved clause; adopted 6 to 0 as Res. 221206-116 ([minutes](https://www.sfmta.com/media/33981/download?inline), PDF p. 11) [primary — read 2026-10-31]. The adopted resolution's code text has §201(c)(17) and no (18) (PDF p. 6) [primary — read 2026-10-31]. So diverters stay a City Traffic Engineer item, which is what the 2023 and 2024 Cayuga and Minnesota approvals show. The codified Transportation Code on American Legal wasn't opened [unretrieved — verify].

**What this means for the asks** [inference — mine]:
- A School Connector of circles, soft diverters or concrete islands on city streets needs no ordinance, no SFMTA Board vote and no state law. It needs a hearing, fire review and money. The kill points are the hearing (comments, withdrawal by staff), SFFD extended review for anything hard, and, for "#" items, a call-up by the Supervisors.
- Making the street a Slow Street, or a hard entry ban under §21101(g), adds an SFMTA Board vote. The Board has done both before (Res. 221206-116; Res. 230117-010).
- No petition or neighbor ballot appears in any of these laws. A Slow Street requires §21101(f) outreach "with notice to abutting residents and owners" (circulation note) [secondary — read 2026-10-31].

## State law

Carried from the circulation plans note, which read california.public.law ("Up to date Verified: Oct. 26, 2026"); leginfo returned 403, so the official text and lettering are [unretrieved — verify]. The staff report quotes §21101(g) in the same words and lettering: "(g) Prohibiting entry to, or exit from, or both, from any street by means of islands, curbs, traffic barriers, or other roadway design features to implement the circulation element of a general plan ... The rules and regulations authorized by this subdivision shall be consistent with the responsibility of local government to provide for the health and safety of its citizens" (PDF p. 5) [primary — read 2026-10-31]. It also cites "section § 21101(f)" for slow streets (PDF p. 4). That is a second source agreeing with the mirror on (f) and (g) as of 2022.

- §21101(f): slow streets "by adopting an ordinance", with six conditions including a finding that "the closure or traffic restriction leaves a sufficient portion of the streets in the surrounding area for other public uses" (circulation note). SF meets the ordinance step with an SFMTA Board resolution under the Charter (circulation note, Charter §8A.102(b)(7)) [primary — read 2026-10-31, in that note].
- §21101.6: "local authorities may not place gates or other selective devices on any street which deny or restrict the access of certain members of the public to the street, while permitting others unrestricted access" (circulation note) [secondary — read 2026-10-31]. SF's diverters exempt "BICYCLES AND EMERGENCY VEHICLES" by sign, and one Page item exempts "LOCAL VEHICLE TRAFFIC, BIKES, EMERGENCY VEHICLES"; how that squares with §21101.6 isn't argued in any record opened [unretrieved — verify].
- **Cameras.** No California statute lets a camera ticket a car for entering a filtered street or making a banned turn; SF's camera powers are §40240 (transit lanes and stops), §40245 (bike lanes) and §22425 (speed) (circulation note, carried from the transit lanes note) [Legistar — read 2026-10-31, in that note]. So camera-enforced filters aren't legal in SF today [inference — mine].

## Fire and emergency access

From the [manual](https://www.sfmta.com/media/45260/download?inline), FY 2025-26, created 2026-01-27, modified 2026-06-16, 62 pp. [primary — read 2026-10-31]:
- **Traffic circles (§9.1.8, p. 32).** "Circular central island intended to lower speeds at minor uncontrolled intersection crossings". Limited review: "Installation of a Traffic Circle with a 2” mountable apron". Extended review if it "Reduces emergency clear width to less than 20’/26’" or "Does not have a mountable apron". "Distance from curb to face of Traffic Circle: 20’ minimum".
- **Diverters (§9.1.9, p. 33).** Limited review for "Soft-Diversion elements as defined by the Slow Streets Design Tool-kit, including soft-hit plastic post traffic diverters and signs". Extended review for "hard traffic diversion elements that apparatuses cannot easily traverse including but not limited to Concrete curbs; Traffic barriers".
- **The manual disagrees with itself on circles.** Figure K (p. 22) lists "Traffic circles" under "Street Design Elements Requiring SFFD Liaison, Battalion Chief, and SFFD Chief Review (1 month)", with no apron exception. §9.1.8 says a circle with a mountable apron gets limited review, which §8 says "typically requires seven days". Both are printed; which governs is a question for SFMTA [unretrieved — verify]. The circulation note reads circles as extended review; the brief reads both.
- **Still open as of 2024-11-07 (Figures P and Q, p. 46).** "Bulb Islands (Slow Streets)": "how far non-mountable bulb islands can extend from the curb into the street". "Pan Basher" barricades: agreed for Shotwell, "Further discussion is needed on whether this design can be applied to all appropriate City streets". "Traffic Diverters (Slow Streets)": "SFMTA would like to pursue a knockdown test to evaluate metal signs as a standard in-roadway treatment, either in a hatched area or on a mountable concrete island".
- **No veto.** When SFFD minimums aren't met, "the departments either reach an agreement on mitigation measures or allow the project to proceed without concurrence, with both departments fully aware of the decision" (p. 12, footnote) [primary — read 2026-10-31]. SFMTA staff "generally avoid proposing projects on SFFD priority response routes" (§4.4.2, p. 13). The Slow Streets page: "MUNI routes and emergency response corridors do not work as Slow Streets" ([program page](https://www.sfmta.com/projects/slow-streets-program)) [primary — read 2026-10-31].

So the design SFFD has already accepted is: soft diverters of posts and signs, circles with a 2 inch mountable apron leaving 20 ft from curb to circle, off priority response routes and Muni lines, and the Shotwell barricade. Concrete diverters go through extended review; Golden Gate at Masonic and Minnesota at 19th got concrete through it. No SFFD response time figure for any SF circle or diverter was found [unretrieved — verify]. No hearing item in the mirror was withdrawn for a fire objection; fire access was raised as a question at Sanchez (2021), Page (2022) and Golden Gate (2025) [inference — mine, from the item texts].

## SF's own before and after, run today

**Slow Streets, injury crashes, longer window.** Same match as above, 16 corridors (Arlington adds none): 153 injury crashes in 2017 to 2019, 90 in 2023 to 2025, down 41%. Citywide: 10,213 to 8,895, down 13% [DataSF ubvf-ztfx — queried 2026-10-31]. The ratio of the two changes is 0.68; with 153 and 90 events the 95% interval runs about 0.52 to 0.88 [inference — mine, Poisson approximation]. In plain words: after Slow Streets went in, crashes on those streets fell about a third more than the city's. Limits:
- It is a bundle: posts, signs, some diverters, later speed humps. It can't separate circles or diverters from the rest.
- The 15 m buffer catches crashes at cross-street intersections, which the street's own treatment may not reach.
- The pandemic cut traffic everywhere; the citywide comparison handles that only if Slow Street blocks recovered at the city's pace.
- Corridors weren't picked for crash history, so regression to the mean is less likely than for resident-requested humps [inference — mine].
- **Nobody counted the boundary streets.** The test below does that.

**What SF measured for diverted traffic.** The 2021 evaluation measured streets next to Slow Streets by volume against road capacity, with a pass mark of "moderate congestion levels (V/C ratio < 0.74)", and found a "Typical average measured hourly V/C ratio: 0.23" (PDF pp. 7, 9) [primary — read 2026-10-31]. That is a congestion test. It doesn't count crashes, speeds or added cars per day on the next street. The 2023 report repeats that Slow Streets "did not negatively impact vehicle traffic on adjacent streets" (PDF p. 8) [primary — read 2026-10-31]. The only SF record of where diverted cars went is qualitative: 26th Street (Shotwell, 2022), Tennessee (Minnesota, 2025, followed by cushions and stop signs), and the October 2020 Fulton left-turn ban that SFMTA held back "due to the Slow Street on Cabrillo" (hearing 2020-10-30) [primary — read in repo mirror 2026-10-31]. This is reader 3's strongest point and the city has no answer to it on file.

**Traffic circles, crude pass.** Ten circles with install dates from 2008 to 2022: 8 injury crashes within 25 m in the three years before, 7 in the three after; citywide crashes fell 5% over the same windows [DataSF bp3t-bd4t, ubvf-ztfx — queried 2026-10-31]. Too few events to say anything. SF has too few circles for its own test; the effect has to come from Seattle and the literature (evidence lane).

## The test to run in SF

**Data.**
- [hkz3-itiu](https://data.sfgov.org/d/hkz3-itiu) Slow Street blocks and install dates (frozen since 2024-01-18).
- [bp3t-bd4t](https://data.sfgov.org/d/bp3t-bd4t) circles, islands and diverter-like devices; [abhw-ffzx](https://data.sfgov.org/d/abhw-ffzx) mid-block devices.
- The hearing mirror for every "EXCEPT BICYCLES" turn restriction and "NO THROUGH TRAFFIC" item, with dates, as the treatment list for diverters, which no dataset carries.
- [ubvf-ztfx](https://data.sfgov.org/d/ubvf-ztfx) injury crashes and [nwes-mmgh](https://data.sfgov.org/d/nwes-mmgh) victims, for age (5 to 17) and walking or biking.
- [3psu-pn9h](https://data.sfgov.org/d/3psu-pn9h) street network, to find each treated street's parallel streets and boundary arterials.
- [7e7j-59qk](https://data.sfgov.org/d/7e7j-59qk) schools.
- [nuek-vuh3](https://data.sfgov.org/d/nuek-vuh3) fire and EMS calls, which carry `received_dttm`, `dispatch_dttm`, `on_scene_dttm` and `case_location`, updated daily. That lets us compare response times to addresses on and beyond treated blocks before and after.
- SFMTA's traffic count CSVs (not DataSF; `85_PERC_SPEED`, `VOLUME_ADT` by CNN, 2014 to 2022) for before counts on the boundary streets (sources note).

**Design.**
- Treated: each Slow Street corridor and each diverter, dated. Spillover ring: the parallel residential streets within two blocks and the boundary arterials. Control: residential streets matched on class, length, district, speed limit and 2015 to 2019 crash count, outside any ring.
- Outcome: injury crashes per year, all and children walking or biking, inside, in the ring and on the arterials, as an event study with segment and year effects, so the whole area's change is the headline and the treated street's is the detail.
- Fire: median and 90th percentile `on_scene_dttm` minus `dispatch_dttm` for calls on treated blocks and in the ring, before and after, against the same station's calls elsewhere.
- Volume: the sunshine ask. SFMTA collects 48-hour counts for each Slow Street; only PDFs are public. Ask for the raw tube counts, with the boundary streets counted in the same weeks.
- Power: about 30 injury crashes a year on these corridors. Three years either side detects a 30% change on the corridors; children alone are too few, so report them as counts, not rates [inference — mine].

Thank you for drafting the [Safe School Connections Plan](https://www.sfmta.com/safe-school-connections-draft-approach-september-2026). I want every trip, for kids, caregivers and everyone else, to be safer, more joyful and more convenient.  
The plan is a good start, but the city needs to deliver faster, with more ambition, accountability and budget to match.

With the changes below, by 2030 the city can:

- Cut the number of kids hurt during school trips by 20%
- Get close to Safe Routes' own goal of 30% of school trips in single-family cars
- Save time for the parents of about 4,000 kids who no longer have to do the school run themselves

You can do all of this with a budget of approximately \$15M a year in infrastructure and \$10M in programs.

Thank you for considering these changes!

## Main Gaps in the Plan

- **No measurable targets or dates**  
  The plan proposes detailed solutions and priorities before it sets clear outcomes.
- **Every school should be considered, not just those near the network**  
  Prioritizing by schools a block from the network ties your hands too soon.
- **School Connectors work better if there's a network to connect to**  
  Kids need a safe route all the way from home to school, or else they won't bike and roll.
- **List of tools is too limited and missing top SFCTA recommendations**  
  At least half a dozen cost-effective tools are missing, and most work at any school regardless of whether it's one block from the network.

## Recommended Plan Changes Backed by Data

With Claude's help, I spent a few days on San Francisco's public data, published studies from other cities, and other city departments' recommendations that haven't been implemented yet.

### Set Measurable Targets

- **Reduce the number of kids hurt in traffic on weekdays from 6AM to 6PM by 20% by 2030**  
  In any travel mode, from about 107 kids a year (2021-22 to 2024-25)
  - This supports the Mayor's [Street Safety Initiative](https://www.sf.gov/street-safety-initiative) and the Board's [Street Safety Act](https://sfgov.legistar.com/View.ashx?M=F&ID=14821038&GUID=E014E308-33EC-4EEF-B09B-CB0EEC48A3C2)
  - Safe Routes' 50% is the right long term goal. 20% by 2030 is the first step. My data says it's achievable with strong execution.
    - Nearly half of these kids are hurt more than 1/4 mile from a school, so changes near schools can only go so far
- **Reduce school trips made in single-family cars to 30% of school trips by 2030**  
  - Down from 48.6% in 2024-25
  - Good alternatives also mean much less traffic for parents who still drive
  - [30% is Safe Routes' own goal, a stretch but possible with excellent execution](https://www.sfsaferoutes.org/documents/2026/SFSRTS_Report_final_spreads_low_res.pdf)
- **Reduce number of caregivers who need to take their kid to school by 10% by 2030**  
  - It answers caregivers' top two complaints in [SFCTA’s 2023 survey](https://www.sfcta.org/sites/default/files/2024-07/SF_School_Access_Plan_Report_2023-05-04_FINAL.pdf#page=33): school trips take too long and are at an inconvenient time.

### Deliver Outcomes Faster

- **Deliver outcomes at 10 pilot schools this school year, with budget of \$250k per school**  
  - Pick 10 pilots spread across the city, including high injury and equity-priority areas like the southeast, Excelsior, Mission, and Sunset
  - Meet the car mode share and caregiver targets at these schools by June 2027
  - Track a leading indicator, like parents' near-miss reports and sense of safety, instead of waiting years for injury counts
  - Start where the school community and district supervisor are ready
- **Roll out individual changes faster**  
  - Once pilots show a tool works, or where evidence elsewhere is strong
  - e.g. School Streets: There's good evidence they work, e.g. a [5.3 point drop in car trips at 166 UK primary schools](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12964863/)
  - e.g. Meet the Mayor's [commitment to phase out 25 mph school zone speed limits at more than 230 schools by 2028](https://www.sf.gov/news-mayor-lurie-takes-steps-to-make-streets-safer-for-san-francisco-children-ahead-of-first-day-of-school)
- **Get more community feedback**  
  - Ask families, neighbors, merchants and advocates before the first change, then report progress and ask again every month

### Investigate a Wider Palette of Effective Tools

Your list of tools isn't enough to reach these goals. These would help:

- **Do SFCTA's top-tier basics** ([School Access Plan, p. 39](https://www.sfcta.org/sites/default/files/2024-07/SF_School_Access_Plan_Report_2023-05-04_FINAL.pdf#page=39))
  - Walk audits at 20 schools a year, about \$1M a year
  - Pickup and drop-off guidance for every school
  - Transportation coordinators at school sites, as SFCTA's caregivers asked
    - They connect families to carpools, bike pools, walking school buses, explain each school's drop off rules, and pass feedback to SFMTA
    - An app built by volunteer SF parents, like Solve SF for school trips, could help each coordinator reach more families.
    - Aim to shift 4% of school trips out of single-family cars
- **Grow Muni ridership, as SFCTA recommends**  
  - Muni training for K-5 kids, via Safe Routes coordinator
  - Make sure families know about discounted fares, then expand them
    - e.g. free Muni for a caregiver riding with a kid to or from school, about \$1.1M a year in new riders' fares plus the fares caregivers already pay. Of caregivers who find the school trip stressful, 21% named its cost, close to the 25% who named safety ([SFCTA, p. 33](https://www.sfcta.org/sites/default/files/2024-07/SF_School_Access_Plan_Report_2023-05-04_FINAL.pdf#page=33))
  - Use data to pick more routes for MTAP to expand to
  - Aim to shift 2% to 4% of school trips to Muni
- **Fund an expansion of SFUSD's yellow bus service, as SFCTA recommends**  
  - This could shift 2% to 4% of school trips, depending on how many seats SFUSD adds

My data also points to bikeways:

- **Build out key segments of Biking and Rolling Plan**  
  - Prioritize segments that give the most kids who now ride in cars a safe bike trip (less than 1000 feet of gap on all-ages and abilities bikeways and within 5 minutes of how long a car trip takes)
  - Sorted this way, the top 25 miles take about 4 times as many kids out of single-family cars per dollar as School Connectors to schools within a block of the network.
  - About \$6M a year of the \$15M builds about 50 miles of these segments by 2030.
  - Top segments include:
    - 30th Avenue in the Outer Richmond
    - 41st Avenue in the Sunset
    - Huron Avenue in the Outer Mission
    - 23rd Street in Noe Valley

Thank you for considering these changes.
